\paragraph{Strong scaling.}
One MPI rank and explicit socket/core-ordered OpenMP placement were used for
three independent runs per physical thread count. For the FP32 DWF4 kernel,
fixed $L=24/32$ throughput grew from 21.6/20.4 to 352.6/350.6\,GFLOP/s
on DINE (1 to 32 physical cores; 51.0/53.7\% efficiency). On DINE2, it grew
from 35.2/32.5 to 768.9/695.4\,GFLOP/s (1 to 64 cores;
34.1/33.5\% efficiency). DINE2's higher absolute throughput has lower
per-core scaling efficiency at the largest tested counts.

\paragraph{Weak scaling.}
With Grid's fifth dimension held fixed, doubling $L$ from 24 to 48 increases
the four-dimensional volume by $16\times$. The matched 2-to-32-thread
comparison therefore holds $L^4/T$ constant and retains 56.4\% of per-core
throughput on DINE versus 32.1\% on DINE2. Weak scaling also changes cache
residency and socket placement, so it cannot by itself isolate a cause.

% Place under \section{Grid vs.\ LIKWID and the roofline}.
\paragraph{Measured roofline.}
LIKWID full-node FP32 peaks were 3341/9287\,GFLOP/s and non-temporal
DRAM roofs 231/398\,GB/s (DINE/DINE2), giving empirical ridge points
14.49/23.34 FLOP/byte. Simultaneous whole-benchmark counters recorded
259.3/505.9\,GFLOP/s and 164.7/243.6\,GB/s, with aggregate intensities
1.57/2.08 SP FLOP/DRAM byte, below the ridge. These measurements include
setup, multiple lattice sizes and non-DWF kernels; they do not give the
operational intensity of an isolated $L=24$ or $L=32$ DWF kernel.

\paragraph{Grid axpy vs. LIKWID.}
At $L=64$ (3\,GiB working set) full-node FP64 Grid axpy reached
174.4/350.4\,GB/s, or 97.6/89.1\% of separately measured temporal
LIKWID triad useful-byte bandwidth (178.6/393.2\,GB/s). Grid's binary
GiB/s were converted to decimal GB/s. This ratio compares different kernels
and working sets; it is not hardware bandwidth utilisation.
